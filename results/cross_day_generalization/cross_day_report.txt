======================================================================
FEATURE 17 - CROSS-DAY GENERALIZATION & MULTI-DAY TRAINING
======================================================================
Cross-day generalization was evaluated on the available compatible
CSE-CIC-IDS2018 days. This is an ADDITIVE research experiment - Features
1-16, L1, L2.x, and L3.1/L3.2 are frozen and untouched (verified, see
Section 11).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
RESEARCH QUESTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
RQ1: How well does the existing Thursday-trained CyberChess model
     generalize to previously unseen traffic days?
RQ2: Does training the same model family on multiple traffic days improve
     performance on a completely held-out day?

----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DATASETS / DAYS USED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
4 days were eligible (Benign=0/Attack=1 binary semantics, 80-column
schema): Thursday-01-03-2018 (original training day), Wednesday-28-02-2018,
Wednesday-14-02-2018, Wednesday-21-02-2018.

Tuesday-20-02-2018 was excluded per instruction: its raw schema has 84
columns (extra Flow ID/Src IP/Src Port/Dst IP) and has never been mapped
or validated against the 65-feature contract - it was never downloaded or
prepared for this project.

See dataset_summary.csv for exact row/window/sequence counts, timestamp
ranges, and preserved caveats for every day. Key facts:
  - Wednesday-28-02-2018: 606,982 cleaned rows, 31,854 windows, 22,006
    sequences, 22.48% attack windows. No major caveat.
  - Wednesday-14-02-2018: raw file has exactly 1,048,575 rows (one less
    than Excel's row limit - truncation signature), 21.5% exact-duplicate
    rate, a corrupted 1970-01-05 timestamp present in raw data. EXCLUDED
    from Experiment B training (see Section 3); still evaluated in
    Experiment A.
  - Wednesday-21-02-2018: raw file also exactly 1,048,575 rows (same
    truncation signature), timestamp range ends at 10:43:21 (covers only
    ~8h47m), extremely bursty traffic (mean 316.7 flows/window, max 811) -
    a volumetric DDoS flood.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. EXPERIMENT A - FROZEN BASELINE (inference only, no retraining)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The existing frozen Thursday-trained models (LogReg, RandomForest, LSTM,
next-state oracle/full-pipeline) were re-evaluated on all 3 external days
via .transform()/.predict() only - reproducing Feature 16's own numbers
exactly (verified: Wednesday-28-02-2018 full-pipeline recall=0.8228,
precision=0.2770, ROC-AUC=0.5649, FPR=0.7668 - matches the known baseline
to 4 decimal places) and extending them with Brier score, degenerate-
prediction flags, median/P95/max absolute forecasting error, per-feature
breakdown, and outlier-dominated-dimension flags that Feature 16's
original run did not compute.

Full-pipeline (LSTM-predicted-state -> classifier) binary results:
  Wednesday-28-02-2018: recall=0.8228 precision=0.2770 roc_auc=0.5649 fpr=0.7668
  Wednesday-14-02-2018: recall=0.6613 precision=0.4036 roc_auc=0.5825 fpr=0.6180
  Wednesday-21-02-2018: recall=0.7447 precision=0.9859 roc_auc=0.7995 fpr=0.2375

RQ1 ANSWER: Generalization is inconsistent and day-dependent. The frozen
Thursday model shows moderate-to-high recall on all 3 external days
(0.66-0.82) but combined with a persistently high false-positive rate on
the two days sharing Thursday's attack type or a related profile
(Wed-28: FPR=0.77; Wed-14: FPR=0.62). Wed-21 (volumetric DDoS, extreme
class imbalance at 95.7% positive) shows a deceptively strong precision
(0.99) that is largely an artifact of class imbalance, not evidence of
transfer to DDoS specifically - ROC-AUC (0.80) is the more informative
number there. No claim of "good generalization" is supportable across all
3 days simultaneously.

See experiment_a_frozen_baseline.json, forecasting_metrics.csv,
attack_progression_metrics.csv, confusion_matrices/experiment_a_*.json for
full detail (all 4 model families x 3 days x 4 horizons).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. EXPERIMENT B - MULTI-DAY TRAINING
----------------------------------------------------------------------
STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE PROTOCOL EXECUTED (not a full 4-day leave-one-day-out):
Two folds were trained and evaluated, both EXCLUDING Wednesday-14-02-2018
from any training combination due to its documented truncation/duplicate
caveat (21.5% duplicate rate, corrupted timestamps) - a real data-quality
concern, not merely a computational-cost decision, though the exclusion
also kept total training time tractable within this session. Wed-14
remains fully evaluated as a frozen-baseline day in Experiment A.

  FOLD fold_holdout_Wed21: TRAIN=[Thursday-01-03-2018, Wednesday-28-02-2018]
    (44,040 combined training sequences), HOLDOUT=Wednesday-21-02-2018
    (1,870 sequences, completely isolated - see leakage_audit.json).
  FOLD fold_holdout_Wed28: TRAIN=[Thursday-01-03-2018, Wednesday-21-02-2018]
    (23,904 combined training sequences), HOLDOUT=Wednesday-28-02-2018
    (22,006 sequences, completely isolated).

A full 4-day leave-one-day-out (4 folds, each excluding one day) was NOT
performed in this session - each fold takes several minutes end-to-end
(data prep + preprocessing fit + LogReg/RF/LSTM/classifier training), and
a 3rd/4th fold would require including Wednesday-14-02-2018 in training,
which was excluded on data-quality grounds above. This is documented, not
silently reduced - no fold was fabricated.

For every fold: preprocessing was fit ONLY on the training days' window
feature vectors (fresh unfitted clone of the Feature 4 template,
data/processed/model_ready/preprocessing_pipeline.joblib, fit via a single
.fit() call - never the frozen Feature 7 artifact, never touched by
writing); LogReg (max_iter=2000, class_weight=balanced, solver=lbfgs),
RandomForest (n_estimators=300, class_weight=balanced), LSTM (Feature 10's
exact architecture: LSTM(68,128,2 layers,dropout=0.2)->Linear(128,68),
Adam lr=1e-3, batch_size=128, MSELoss, max_epochs=50/patience=7), and the
next-state classifier (LogisticRegression on 68D state, Feature 11's
philosophy) were all trained ONLY on the training days' data. Random seed:
42 (Python/NumPy/PyTorch), recorded per fold in configs/*.json. The
classification threshold was NOT tuned - fixed at 0.50 throughout (see
compute_classification_metrics()'s default).

RESULTS (full-pipeline binary metrics, held-out day):
  fold_holdout_Wed21 (train=Thu+Wed28): recall=0.9894 precision=0.9568
    roc_auc=0.8082 fpr=1.0000 (!)
  fold_holdout_Wed28 (train=Thu+Wed21): recall=0.5832 precision=0.3177
    roc_auc=0.6078 fpr=0.4471

IMPORTANT DEGENERATE-BEHAVIOR FLAGS (automated, not overlooked):
  - RandomForest, BOTH folds, showed severely degraded or fully degenerate
    behavior: fold_holdout_Wed21's RF predicted recall=0.0/precision=0.0
    (flagged degenerate=True by the automated check - it predicted
    essentially all-negative on the held-out day).
  - fold_holdout_Wed21's full-pipeline classifier achieved high recall
    (0.9894) ONLY by also reaching FPR=1.0 - i.e. it is close to
    predicting almost everything positive on a day that is itself 95.7%
    positive; this recall figure is NOT evidence of good detection and is
    explicitly flagged here as such, per instruction not to describe a
    higher F1/recall as an improvement without checking FPR and class
    balance.

RQ2 ANSWER: Training on multiple days did NOT uniformly improve
performance, and in one case (RandomForest) made it categorically worse
(degenerate all-negative prediction). The two folds moved in OPPOSITE
directions relative to their respective frozen baselines (see Section 4) -
multi-day training is not shown to be a reliable improvement strategy for
this model family on these days, though it did not need to be, since
evidence here is genuinely mixed, not negative-only, for the LogReg model
on the fold_holdout_Wed21 comparison. Do not read this as evidence that
multi-day training helps OR hurts in general - only that it changed
results, non-uniformly, on the 2 folds tested.

See fold_holdout_Wed21_results.json, fold_holdout_Wed28_results.json,
fold_summary.csv, trained_models/fold_*/ for full detail and saved
artifacts (kept separate from Features 1-16's frozen models).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SAME-HELD-OUT-DAY COMPARISON (frozen Thursday-only vs multi-day)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Neutral differences only - no winner declared. Full detail in
model_comparison.csv (32 metric rows x 2 folds).

Held-out = Wednesday-21-02-2018:
  Full-pipeline: Frozen F1=0.8485, FPR=0.2375 | Multi-day F1=0.9728, FPR=1.0000
    Difference: F1 changed by +0.1243; FPR changed by +0.7625 (much worse)
  Multi-day training changed recall/F1 upward but changed FPR sharply
  upward too (degenerate-adjacent behavior, see Section 3).

Held-out = Wednesday-28-02-2018:
  Full-pipeline: Frozen F1=0.4144, FPR=0.7668 | Multi-day F1=0.4113, FPR=0.4471
    Difference: F1 changed by -0.0031 (essentially unchanged); FPR changed
    by -0.3197 (substantially improved)
  Multi-day training changed FPR substantially downward with F1 essentially
  unchanged - a genuinely different, arguably more balanced operating
  point, not simply "worse" or "better" on a single number.

5. WORLD MODEL FORECASTING RESULTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
K=1/2/3/5 MSE/RMSE/MAE/median/P95 for every day and fold are in
forecasting_metrics.csv. Headline K=1 one-step MAE:
  Experiment A (frozen): Wed-28=0.499, Wed-14=0.408, Wed-21=14.586 (!)
  fold_holdout_Wed21 (multi-day, holdout=Wed-21): MAE=7.517 (LOWER error
    than frozen's 14.586 on the SAME held-out day - a genuine improvement
    on this specific metric)
  fold_holdout_Wed28 (multi-day, holdout=Wed-28): MAE=0.405 (essentially
    unchanged vs frozen's 0.499)

Wed-21's forecasting error is an order of magnitude larger than the other
days in EVERY configuration (frozen or multi-day) - consistent with its
extreme, bursty DDoS traffic profile producing genuinely harder-to-predict
raw feature magnitudes even after scaling. Per-feature outlier-dominated
dimensions are listed explicitly per day/fold in the JSON results (not
hidden) - e.g. fold_holdout_Wed21 flags 20 of 68 dimensions (Active/Idle/
IAT statistics, several count features, Protocol_0) as outlier-dominated
(max error >10x mean error for that feature).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
11. IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  - This is NOT a full 4-day leave-one-day-out (only 2 of 4 possible folds
    were run; Wednesday-14-02-2018 never contributed to any training
    combination, per its documented data-quality caveat).
  - fold_holdout_Wed28 was not independently re-run for determinism
    verification (time constraint).
  - No hyperparameter search was performed for any model in Experiment B -
    Feature 8/9/10/11's original hyperparameters were reused as-is
    ("same general architecture and training philosophy as Feature 10",
    per instruction).
  - Multi-day training's LSTM validation split is a simple per-day
    chronological last-10% holdout, not a principled hyperparameter-tuned
    validation scheme - sufficient for early-stopping only.
  - Results here do NOT establish universal generalization, production
    readiness, real-time detection, actual firewall mitigation, that
    multi-day training necessarily improves performance, that attack
    progression has been proven, or that LSTM forecasting is universally
    accurate. None of these claims are made.
  - The severe FPR degradation seen in fold_holdout_Wed21's full-pipeline
    result, and RandomForest's degenerate behavior in both folds, mean
    NEITHER trained multi-day model should be treated as a drop-in
    improvement over the frozen Thursday-only model without further
    investigation.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
12. DOES THE EVIDENCE SUPPORT FURTHER MODEL WORK?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes, cautiously: the mixed, day-dependent results (Section 4) and the
concrete failure modes surfaced (RandomForest degeneracy, one fold's
FPR collapse) are themselves useful, actionable findings that justify
further investigation (e.g. class-balance-aware training, a principled
validation scheme, and completing the remaining 2 folds of the
leave-one-day-out protocol) - but the evidence gathered here does not
itself demonstrate that multi-day training is a beneficial direction; it
demonstrates that the question is answerable and worth pursuing further
with more folds and more careful handling of class imbalance.

No overall "winner" is declared between the frozen Thursday-only model and
the multi-day-trained models, per instruction.
